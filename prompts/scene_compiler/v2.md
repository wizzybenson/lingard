$targets

## Write
- learner_goal: one short sentence in $language_name, the visible goal (e.g. "Get your deposit back").
- elicitation: for EVERY target above except pronunciation targets, one tactic: a concrete thing the character does or asks that makes using the target natural. Written in English for the actor.
- approved_facts: $facts_min to $facts_max short concrete facts in $language_name. The character can say NOTHING factual beyond these, its own facts and what is written in the place, so a real conversation about this goal must never run out of facts. Imagine the conversation: every question the learner will plausibly ask, and every detail the character would naturally mention, needs a fact. Cover, as far as they apply:
  - the procedure: the steps, in order, and who does what;
  - what is required or accepted (documents, forms, conditions) and what is not;
  - prices, amounts, fees, payment methods;
  - times, dates, durations, deadlines, opening hours;
  - what is available, in stock or possible today, and what is not;
  - where things are in this place, and where to go next;
  - what happens next and its consequences (letters, calls, decisions, refunds);
  - the character's own position, stake and what they will and won't agree to;
  - the limits of what the character knows: one or two things they honestly don't know, and who would.
  Facts must agree with the character's own facts and what is written in the place, and with each other. Original content only: invent plausible local details, never real brands or real people.
- success_conditions: 1–3 observable things the learner must do to achieve the goal.
- evidence_required: for the 1–2 most important grammar or function targets, min_uses (usually 1).
- exits: 1–3 graceful in-world endings with consequences (agreement reached, learner walks out, etc.).
- max_turns: between 10 and 30.
- mood: the character's mood at the start.

Use only the target keys listed above. Keep it realistic, respectful and free of stereotypes.
